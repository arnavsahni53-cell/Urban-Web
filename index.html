The Challenge

Many journeys
in a journey.

Cities are investing in mobility. New modes, new business models, new technologies — with each one expanding what urban movement can be.

Shared mobility Ride-hailing Micro-mobility Last-mile delivery Electric Autonomous Urban aviation

But every investment is building its own world. The traveller is left to connect them alone.

City mobility today

The result is a city of silos — value locked inside individual systems, every new connection starting from scratch.

This shift has happened before

Each one built
the condition.

Not building the thing everyone needed — building the conditions for anyone to build it.

Internet The web did not build websites, social media, or online commerce. It built the open standard that let anyone build anything — and reach anyone else. Infrastructure of Information
GPS GPS did not build navigation or routing apps. It built the positioning layer that maps, logistics, and ride-hail all grew from. Infrastructure of Location
Mobile Networks Mobile networks did not build services. They built the universal standard that let any device, any provider, connect to any other. Infrastructure of Communication
Urban Web brings this shift to cities.

A program by

Networks for Humanity (NFH) is a global non-profit initiative building open, interoperable and decentralised digital infrastructure that enables broader access and participation in the digital economy. Their shared network powers Urban Web, providing the open standard that any mobility service can connect to.

WRI is a global research organisation working to improve people's lives, protect nature and stabilise the climate by leveraging data, expertise and global reach to influence policy and catalyse change. They bring city relationships, policy expertise, and on-the-ground research that makes Urban Web work in practice.
