Four ideas.
One fundamental shift.

Every network that changed the world worked this way.

01
Join once.

A single connection to Urban Web makes any mobility service immediately reachable by every other service on the network. Not a connection to each partner — a connection to the shared layer.

02
Reach everything.

The moment a service joins, every other service on the network becomes reachable — simultaneously. Not one by one. All at once. That simultaneity is the point.

03
Grow beyond mobility.

As more services join, the network expands. Commerce, logistics, energy — the city's mobility layer becomes a node in a larger network of networks.

04
Nothing in the middle.

Services connect directly — peer to peer. Urban Web is the standard, not the intermediary. No platform owns the connection. No single organisation controls the flow.

Not just what you build today.
What others build tomorrow.

The value of open infrastructure is not what you build on it. It's what others build, without asking permission.

The obvious unlock

When every mobility service in a city speaks the same standard, any two of them can work together without a bilateral agreement, a custom build, or a procurement cycle. A service that joins can immediately reach every other service that has joined. The network grows without anyone coordinating its growth. Every new participant makes it more useful for every participant already in it.

The deeper point

The more important aspect is what nobody planned. When everything sits on a shared open layer, the conditions exist for solutions that nobody has thought of yet. Because the infrastructure is open and machine-readable, it is ready for the next generation of AI-native interfaces — with agents that discover, transact, and coordinate across a city without being trapped inside any platform.

A program by

Networks for Humanity (NFH) is a global non-profit initiative building open, interoperable and decentralised digital infrastructure. Their shared network powers Urban Web, providing the open standard that any mobility service can connect to.

WRI is a global research organisation working to improve people's lives, protect nature and stabilise the climate. They bring city relationships, policy expertise, and on-the-ground research that makes Urban Web work in practice.
